Not every product needs AI.

Start where the work hurts, then ask two questions: how much is it worth, and how ready are we to build it? The near peak, ready now, is where to begin. The far one is the big bet: worth more, and a longer walk.

“We can't get a brand-new Mercedes in here when we have bicycles over there.”

A client, during a discovery project. From my note The value of the discovery phase

2 of 4

The best AI is the one nobody has to ask.

When people have to open a new app and type a prompt, adoption stalls. The agents that change how a company works run in the background: the reply is drafted before you open the email.

How much should they do on their own? Try the ladder ↓

3 of 4

Build a rough one, then measure it.

Discovery, a rough prototype, a measurement, then live. A prototype that works tells you more than a polished deck. My document-extraction prototype at MHR reached 92% accuracy and cut processing from minutes to about thirty seconds, and the number, not the demo, won the budget to build it properly.

4 of 4

What I think happens next.

  1. Companies will stop counting AI pilots and start counting AI in production.
  2. The agents that matter will work inside the tools people already use, not in another chat window.
  3. Audit trails and evaluations will become as routine for AI as tests are for code.

Written in October 2026. I'll check them in a year.

    Lab

    Things I build to learn.

    Small products and prototypes, made in the evenings to find out what AI can really do. The code is on GitHub.

    1. Working prototype

      BookWorm

      A book recommender that can't invent a book.

      It learns your taste from a conversation, and sharpens it if you hand over your library mid-chat: a Goodreads export, or just screenshots of your Kindle shelf. It only recommends titles it has found in Open Library or Google Books, and every shortlist ends with an honest anti-recommendation.

      TypeScript · Next.js · Claude

      Code ↗

    2. Foundations laid

      Wishlist Price Agent

      Tells you when a deal is genuinely good, not just cheaper.

      Built contracts first: a frozen data model that every part is written against, an evaluation slot waiting for each LLM component, and a step-by-step plan a coding agent can follow. The contracts and the plan are done; the model comes next.

      Python · Pydantic · contracts first

      Code ↗

    3. Playable

      Tamagotchi for words

      A virtual pet that teaches children Polish–English vocabulary.

      Twelve-minute sessions, four learning games, spaced repetition with Leitner boxes, ninety-odd words across three levels, and every graphic drawn in code.

      Python · pygame

      Code ↗

    4. Experiments

      Receipts and invoices

      Structured fields out of messy paperwork.

      An experiment in reading receipts, invoices and ID cards: recognise the text, work out what kind of document it is, then pull out the fields with a language model.
